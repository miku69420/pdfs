Relation - Social ties
Relation - a series of social ties (of a particular type) between
group members.

Trust - a positive expectation that the other person will behave according
to your expectations (vs. suspicion, doubt)
Cooperation - an action with multiple actors and a common goal or set of
goals ( vs. conflict)
Tolerance –cohabitation agreement; acceptance and understanding for
those who act differently than you, based on other beliefs or lifestyles (vs.
intolerance)

→ The ties are diverse and can indicate:
The way one person relates to another (e.g., friendship, respect,
condescension/superiority)
The flow of some resources (e.g., circulation of information, goods,
money)
Affiliation (e.g., membership in an association, group, party; institutional
affiliation)
Interaction (e.g., exchange of messages, dialogue, face-to-face meeting,
fight)



Social networks
Social network - a finite set of actors together with the
established relationship or relationships between these actors
(social relationships that have very different content -
information, power, control, etc.)

Actors (individual or group entities) - Nodes
Relationships - Ties

A. Continuous social ties

A1. Similarities • Physical proximity (e.g., neighbourhood neighbours, bank colleagues, next-door
neighbours)

• Co-membership (e.g. members of the same organisation - party, association, etc.)
• Sharing the same values, behaviours, attitudes, beliefs (e.g. support for right-wing

political values, support for the LGBT community, embracing the same religion)
A2. Social Networks • Role relationships (e.g., mother, father, boss, teacher, tutor, mentor, etc.)

• Cognitive/emotional relationships (e.g., I am friends with X, I like Y, I know Z, I don't
like W, etc.

B. Discreet social ties

B1. Interactions Talk with...; Dine with...; Go to party with...; Fight with... etc.

B2. Flows Resources that flow from one network node (individual) to another in succession
(e.g. a sum of money, a book, a piece of information) or simultaneously (e.g. an e-mail 
can reach several nodes simultaneously). 

Borgatti andi Lopez-Kidwell, 2012 cited in Hâncean (2014, p.39)



Types of social networks

• Dyad: two actors and their possible relationships [basic unit in analysis].     
A        B the characteristics of ties (directional, reciprocity)

multiplex nature (A, B - friends but also co-workers)

• Triad: set of three actors and their possible relationships
A        B Tranzitivity: if A likes B and B likes C, then A will like C.

C Balance: if A and B like each other, then they will be similar in their assessment of C; 

if A and B dislike each other, then they will have different assessments of C.

• One-mode networks – a single set of actors (the communication network
within an organisation)

• 2-mode networks – two sets of actors : affiliate networks - a set of actors
and a set of events

• Ego-network – personal networks: consist of a central node (ego) and all
nodes to which it is connected (alters)

Capital social
Coleman (1988, 1990): social capital is that type of resource which leads to 
the constitution of the public good as a result of the voluntary cooperation of 
individuals, which allows access to resources controlled by others, which 
reduces the transaction costs by establishing trust and reciprocity between 
partners, and which provides rapid information exchange.

Social capital is a characteristic of the social structure, which is manifested 
through trust and relationships that allow individuals, communities and 
societies to access and mobilize resources and information that would 
otherwise be removed from them and use them in individual, community 
and societal development projects (Voicu, 2015, p. 61)

→ Social capital = resources (material, informational, symbolic) that you get 
from social relations (of different intensities) and that you use for personal 
(individual) or group purposes - based on trust and reciprocity

Bonding
• Bonding social networks→ Bonding social capital : 

manifests itself through trust and relationships placed
at the level of the primary group or groups of 
belonging - it ensures the cohesion of these groups
and contributes to the creation of a familiar 
environment in which access to goods and information
is guaranteed. (Voicu, 2015, p.60)

• strong ties - e.g. between members of the same 
family or within an ethnic group; this type of social 
capital is about the day-to-day life of everyone and
is indispensable for living (getting by); 

• „to trusting and co-operative relations between
members of a network who see themselves as 
being similar, in terms of their shared social 
identity” (Szreter și Woolcock, 2004, p.654)



Bridging
• Bridging social networks → bridging social capital: is 

that which manifests itself through participation in 
associations, through frequent meetings with friends, 
through generalized trust (in all people, not only in 
specific groups) and through trust in institutions; it 
unites through trust and relationships individuals 
from different groups, allowing them to develop 
common actions, to access things and to learn 
information under the control of people much 
different from those in the group of primary 
belonging (e.g., family) - (Voicu, 2015, pp.60-61)

• weaker, less dense but overlapping and 
interlocking ties - e.g. business partners, circle of 
acquaintances, friends, friends of friends, 
generally contacts between people belonging to 
less close groups; it is this type of social capital 
that favours progress (getting ahead), by putting 
into motion social energies in different areas and 
groups 

• comprises relations of respect and mutuality 
between people who know that they are not 
alike in some socio-demographic (or social 
identity) sense (differing by age, ethnic group, 
class, etc)- Szreter și Woolcock, 2004, p. 655

Linking

• Linking social networks→ Linking social 
capital: refers to connections between
individuals and groups of individuals at different
levels of influence and social status; for 
example, connections between the political 
elite and the general public or between
individuals belonging to different social classes

• the ability to draw on and manage
resources, ideas and information from
institutions beyond the community - is
