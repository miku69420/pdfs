a)Exposure: People must be exposed to your

c)Motivation: call to action - experiment to

well.

• People must be keen for information.

deal with their hopes, fears, animosities and so 
on.

• Among the reasons people are motivated to spread 
others' content/ideas are: personal relevance, 
humour, usefulness, and shared common interest.

Berger and Milkman (2012)
• analysed articles in the New York Times over a three-

month period to identify which types of articles were 
distributed most often and why.

• They examined each article to identify the emotions the 
article evoked.

• While articles that evoked positive emotions were 
shared more than negative ones on average, negative 
emotions, such as anxiety and anger, increased sharing.

→There are certain types of emotions with high arousal, 
whether positive or negative, that compel sharing.

• Sadness is a low arousal emotion.

• Awe ( amazement), anger and anxiety are high-arousal 
emotions.



Why do people spread fake
news?(Talwar et al. 2019)
• Online trust, self-disclosure, FoMO (fear of 

missing out) and social media fatigue have 
been positively associated with the distribution

• In terms of online social comparison, there was 
a negative association with fake news

• Self-disclosure and social media fatigue were 
positively associated with checking the

• Online trust was negatively associated with 
verifying the authenticity of news before

Quesenberry and Coolsen (2019)
• examined the text of posts that brands made on their Facebook pages to see what 

content increased likes, shares, comments and, ultimately, organic reach. 

• Since organic reach for Facebook page content has declined in recent years, it's 
important to figure out what type of content users are willing to share because 
Facebook's algorithm is more likely to give us content that our friends engage with. 

• Conducted a content analysis of the text in 1,000 Facebook brand posts to find out what 
increases engagement metrics that drive organic reach (e.g. likes, shares, comments)

• focused on 18 variables: Aspects of posts such as photos and links, character and word 
length, number of hashtags, number of links and number of brand mentions, content of 
posts - new/current style posts, posts focused on promotions or pricing, contests and 
sweepstakes, posts, posts on corporate social responsibility (CSR) and social causes, 
events, celebrity-focused posts, questions, calls to action, posts that used exclamation 
points, fan contests and user-generated content, educational posts, and posts 
highlighting specific times or dates.

• They found that: brand posts on Facebook that indicated something was new received 
more shares and comments. Posts that emphasized a date or time, such as a deadline to 
complete an action, received more shares. Educational posts, on the other hand, 
received fewer likes and comments. Interestingly, promotional posts, contest posts and 
posts related to social or CSR causes were not associated with any of the three 
engagement metrics: shares, likes and comments.



Shahbaznezhad, H., Dolan, R., & Rashidirad, M. (2021). The Role of Social Media 
Content Format and Platform in Users’ Engagement Behavior. Journal of 
Interactive Marketing, 53(1), 47-65. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intmar.2020.05.001

• The level of engagement varies from simple types of engagement (e.g., "liking" a post on 
Instagram) to larger types of customer engagement in co-creation activities (e.g., posting 
reviews).

• Instagram users (fans) appear to be more passively engaged, compared to Facebook fans, who 
tend to demonstrate more active behaviour in the form of comments →Facebook can typically 
be used on a desktop device versus the mobile app, allowing users to more easily type 
comments compared to those on their phone → if a goal of social media content is to collect 
rich feedback through comments and discussion, this finding suggests that Facebook may be a 
more suitable platform.

• When posting content strategically, using the 'photo' format will generate significantly more 
likes than comments. This can be a powerful tool for boosting the 'virality' of a post, inspiring 
social media managers to consider the impact of visual content on engagement. 

• In the case of emotional content, the use of video format will stimulate active user engagement 
(in the form of comments). Travel companies that might want to communicate a strong 
emotional message and get active engagement (comments) should, therefore, use videos in 
their content.

• The number of comments on a post and the sentiment of these comments significantly boost 
the number of likes on the post → positive and active engagement in the form of comments 
increases the continued virality of the post in the form of likes. 

• Fans tend to express their opinion and write more comments with a negative sentiment than a 
positive one. Managers, as well as researchers, need to better understand the implications of 
negative comments and word-of-mouth on social media platforms, as this could be potentially 
damaging to a brand.

The power of social media 
platforms
• This focus on platforms represents a significant shift from the 

relatively decentralised online ecosystem that characterised 
Western digital media from the late 1990s to the middle of the first 
decade of this century, in which a variety of competing social 
networking services and DIY websites or blogs coexisted on a 
single, common platform (the World Wide Web), itself built on the 
public Internet.

• The digital evolution that has been achieved by expanding the 
reach and connections between social media platforms and 
creating data streams and interfaces with other websites has led to 
"the rise of [the] [digital] platform [model] as the dominant 
infrastructural and economic model of the social web" (Helmond, 
2015, p. 1). 

• This 'platformisation' signifies not only that platforms such as 
YouTube, Facebook, or Instagram have great power in the 
information and creative content industries but also that their 
logic—their mode of operation and their value systems (editorial 
politics)—are reshaping society and culture more profoundly 
(Burgess 2015; Cunningham and Craig, 2021). 

• Content creators have played a major role in building cultures and 
audiences on platforms, but it is also true that platforms have the 
power to transform or disrupt the careers of creators. 

• Platform logics shape what counts as value (for example, audience 
attention or engagement) and how that value is measured 
(whether by clicks, subscriptions, viewing time, or a combination of 
these).



The power of social media 
platforms

• Due to their ability to create communities where users feel 
more connected to influencers through higher levels of 
engagement, authenticity, and trustworthiness, social media 
celebrities have an advantage when it comes to promoting 
products: social media celebrities' degree of influence on 
consumers is higher due to more effective audience 
penetration and more effective influence to buy (Nouri, 
2018). 

• Substantially captures consumers' attention and spending to 
drive higher sales and draw more attention to brands

• Current trends in corporate social media platforms (e.g., 
Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, TikTok, Youtube) have been to 
adopt a much less open and centralized approach to 
innovation geared towards a policy of growing user data 
markets, which has made open access to data less 
applicable. 

•  because of their cultural power, their role in extreme 
discourses, and the rise of far-right subcultures, as well as 
growing concerns about the exploitation of user data for 
commercial purposes without regard for privacy, SM 
platforms should be carefully studied and scrutinised (not 
only by researchers but also by authorities)

Social Media Listening vs Social 
Media Tracking

• Social media listening -why your audience says certain 
things about or related to your brand in SM. 

•  Social media tracking - how they tell them.

• Social media listening - a great way to track what customers 
and potential customers have to say about your business, 
product/service, content, support and more; involves 
monitoring your brand's social media profiles for customer 
feedback, direct mentions of your brand, and any 
conversations with relevant keywords, topics, competitors 
or industries → then the results are analysed and the 
findings help determine the best ways to improve your 
social media strategy, brand awareness and SM presence.

• Social media tracking is often also referred to as social 
media monitoring. It involves identifying and responding to 
mentions of the brand (keyword) on social media through 
comments, direct messages, and more. Social media 
monitoring is the first step in the process of understanding 
what audiences think and say about a brand on social 
media.

Sentiment analysis

• There's so much data posted daily on social media that it 
can be hard to keep up. Not only do you want to know 
what's being said about your own business, but you want to 
know what's being said about your competitors, the 
industry sector and emerging trends among your 
customers. You want to be in the know.

• Social media sentiment analysis is the process of 
interpreting and determining whether text data collected in 
social media is positive, negative or neutral. This analysis is 
not limited to simply collecting and counting the number of 
mentions, comments or hashtags.

• Sentiment analysis doesn't just collect and count mentions, 
comments, or hashtags. It goes deeper, providing a 
profound understanding of the attitudes, opinions, and 
emotions behind the text. For instance, a post collected 
from Facebook might mention your brand in a positive or 
negative light  , offering valuable context beyond mere 
numbers. This depth of understanding can make you feel 
more enlightened and knowledgeable about your brand's 
perception.

• Sentiment analysis gives way to an even deeper analysis of 
MS mentions. For example, once we know that the 
predominant underlying sentiment is negative, we can look 
deeper for causes and take action.
